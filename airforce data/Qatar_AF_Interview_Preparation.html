PHASE 2 — Technical Questions
Q7 What is your trade and what does it involve?
"My trade is RO FITT G — Radio Officer Fitter Ground. It involves operation, maintenance, inspection, installation, and repair of ground-based radio communication systems, navigation aids, and avionics ground support equipment used in support of military aviation operations."
Q8 Explain your Flight Line experience.
"At No. 850 Readiness Flight, PAF Base Farid, I operated the Ground Entry Station supporting AWACS operations. When the Swedish AWACS aircraft took off, its radar data was downlinked to our GES. I maintained the full data relay chain — M3SR Radios, Ground Entry Module, Cisco Catalyst Switch 3560, Digital Timing System, LMR Router, and VHF Filter — and ensured this data was forwarded in real time to the designated Air Command and Control Sectors."
Q9 What is a Ground Entry Station (GES)?
"A Ground Entry Station is a ground-based facility that receives downlinked data from airborne surveillance platforms like AWACS. It processes and relays radar picture and intelligence data to air command and control sectors, allowing ground commanders to have real-time air picture from the airborne radar. I operated and maintained all communication equipment within the GES."
Q10 What is AWACS and what was your role with it?
"AWACS stands for Airborne Warning and Control System. It is an airborne radar platform that provides 360-degree air surveillance. In PAF we operated Swedish AWACS aircraft. My role was on the ground — at the Ground Entry Station — where I received the downlinked radar data from the aircraft, maintained all equipment in the data link chain, and ensured continuous relay of information to Air Command Sectors."
Q11 Describe your Quality Control experience.
"At PAF Base Faisal, No. 107 AED Quality Control Squadron, I performed pre and post maintenance quality control inspection of all avionics equipment. I supervised avionics technicians, evaluated defect trends, initiated corrective actions, maintained technical documentation, and liaised with flight operations on equipment serviceability. I was rated SUPERIOR in trade proficiency and served as technical supervisor for the QC team for nearly 5 years."
Q12 What communication systems have you worked on?
"Throughout my 23 years I worked on VHF and UHF radio systems, M3SR tactical radios, Voice Logging Systems, PDH and SDH optic fiber systems, Defcomm digital switches and exchanges, PABX telephone exchanges including Hicomm 330H, Public Address Systems, LMR routers, VHF filters, Ground Entry Modules, and Digital Timing Systems. I also hold CCNA certification with 92% score covering network infrastructure."
Q13 Have you worked with ATC communications?
"Yes. At PAF Base Kohat Engineering Wing, I served in the Radio Room providing full communication support to Air Traffic Controllers. I maintained all VHF and UHF radio systems used by ATC, Voice Logging System, and supporting infrastructure ensuring safe aircraft operations at the base."
Q14 Tell me about the TPS-43G radar.
"The TPS-43G is an American long-range air defence radar. At PAF Base Samungli No. 400 Squadron, the radar data fed into our Radio Room. I maintained the radio communication systems in that room and provided direct communication support to Air Defence Controllers during live operational missions of flying squadrons, ensuring uninterrupted real-time support."
Q15 What is your 2nd Line experience?
"I have two postings of 2nd line experience. At PAF Base Kohat Engineering Wing (4 years), I maintained base-wide communication infrastructure including radio systems, SDH backbone, and Defcomm digital exchange. At PAF Base Nur Khan No. 103 ALC Inspection Section (4 years 5 months), I conducted comprehensive quality inspection of all types of air and ground equipment at the Air Logistics Centre, prepared defect trend reports, and coordinated rectification with technical teams."
Q16 What awards did you receive in PAF?

PHASE 5 — Interview Do's and Don'ts
DO
  • Speak slowly and clearly
  • Use military terminology confidently
  • Mention SUPERIOR rating naturally
  • Say "Sir" when addressing an officer
  • Pause and think before answering
  • Be honest about your experience
  • Sit straight — military posture
  • Ask questions at the end
DON'T
  • Rush your answers
  • Over-explain or ramble
  • Sound boastful or overconfident
  • Use informal or casual language
  • Say "I don't know" — say "I will confirm"
  • Exaggerate or claim false experience
  • Interrupt the interviewer
  • Forget to mention your 5 medals
